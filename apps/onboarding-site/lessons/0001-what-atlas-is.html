Atlas · Lesson 1 · 3 min

Meet Atlas

Atlas helps teams build and run integrations without giving AI direct access to their systems.

You describe what you want to connect. Atlas proposes a workflow using the APIs your team has approved. A person reviews and tests it before it can run.

Business value

The promise

Atlas gives durability to the process across services, not to the code inside each service.

What Atlas adds

  • Automatic retries with backoff for failed API calls.
  • Workflows that survive crashes, restarts, and outages.
  • Resume from the failed step instead of restarting everything.
  • Idempotency keys to reduce duplicate side effects.
  • Timeouts and durable waiting.
  • Ordered multi-service execution.
  • Compensation before irreversible actions.
  • Version pinning for in-flight runs.
  • Execution history, visibility, and controlled repair.

Business value: fewer integration failures, duplicate charges, half-completed processes, and custom retry/recovery code.

What Atlas does not add

You do not get durability inside an API call:

  • Resume midway through the endpoint's logic.
  • Durable internal timers, waits, signals, or child workflows.
  • Automatic replay of internal decisions.
  • Fine-grained retry of individual internal operations.
  • Visibility into internal progress.
  • Compensation for hidden internal side effects.
  • Strong deduplication unless the endpoint supports idempotency.

Temporal sees each endpoint call as one opaque Activity: it succeeded, failed, or timed out. If an endpoint lacks idempotency or compensation, Atlas can detect and manage that risk, but cannot manufacture those guarantees.
